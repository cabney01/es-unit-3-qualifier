8.1 — Human Population Growth

Births and immigration add people; deaths and emigration remove people. Population density = population ÷ area. Read the axes and slopes of population graphs. Review infant mortality and life expectancy.

8.2 — Predicting Population Growth

Total fertility rate is children per woman; replacement level is approximately 2.1. Wide-base population pyramids indicate many young people; narrow-base pyramids may suggest future decline. Review demographic transition, birthrates, and death rates.

8.3 — People and Their Environments

Ecological footprints estimate demand for natural resources. Environmental impact depends on both population and consumption per person. Know sanitation, clean water, renewable-energy benefits, technological tradeoffs, and stewardship.

9.1 — Environmental Health

Distinguish biological, chemical, physical, and social hazards. A hazard can cause harm; exposure is contact with it; risk considers likelihood and severity. Correlation does not necessarily prove causation.

9.2 — Biological and Social Hazards

Know pathogens, hosts, vectors, and routes of transmission. Review handwashing, ventilation, safe food and water, emerging diseases, antimicrobial resistance, and social conditions that affect risk.

9.3–9.4 — Toxic Substances and Natural Disasters

Bioaccumulation occurs in one organism over time; biomagnification increases concentration higher in a food web. Review persistent pollutants, hazard identification, communication, evacuation, and warning plans.
